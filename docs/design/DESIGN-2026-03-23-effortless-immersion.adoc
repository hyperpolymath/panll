== The Motorcycle Principle

Development should feel like riding a motorcycle through the countryside as an
experienced driver. You feel the wind on your face, the sun, the sense of
embodied motion. You move from this to that effortlessly and performantly. You
are not held up fiddling with things. You are enjoying the view and the sense of
love of the craft and how beautiful it is.

Only when you misplace the gear or an emergency arises are you pulled out of it
— and that _is_ necessary, that is the system correctly alerting you — but for
most of your time you are in the experience.

This is the experience of human and machine for the coder. Not the semicolon
gauntlet.

== Theoretical Grounding

=== Csikszentmihalyi's Flow (1990)

Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's concept of _flow_ describes a state of complete
absorption in an activity where:

- **Challenge matches skill** — not too easy (boredom), not too hard (anxiety)
- **Clear goals** — you know what you are trying to do at each moment
- **Immediate feedback** — the system tells you how you are doing, continuously
- **Deep concentration** — distractions are excluded from consciousness
- **Loss of self-consciousness** — the ego dissolves into the activity
- **Transformation of time** — hours feel like minutes
- **Autotelic experience** — the activity is intrinsically rewarding

PanLL should create and protect the _conditions_ for flow, not try to simulate
or gamify it.

=== Social Presence (Short, Williams & Christie, 1976)

The feeling of being _with_ another person even through a mediated channel. When
you are on the phone and it feels like you are together. This applies to PanLL
in two ways:

1. **Presence with the codebase** — the workspace should feel like you are
   _inside_ the project, not looking at it through a window
2. **Presence with collaborators** — when working with AI agents, other
   developers, or the ECHIDNA prover, the interaction should feel like they are
   _here_, not that you are sending messages to a remote service

=== Bio-Psycho-Social Model

Immersion in development is not purely cognitive. It is:

- **Biological** — ergonomic, not fatiguing, respecting circadian and attention
  rhythms, sensory comfort (colours, motion, contrast)
- **Psychological** — clear mental model, predictable behaviour, no anxiety from
  unexplained state changes, autonomy and competence
- **Social** — feeling connected to the broader ecosystem, seeing your work in
  context, understanding who else is affected

== Design Implications for PanLL

=== What Creates Flow

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Principle | PanLL Implementation

| Challenge-skill balance
| Adaptive complexity — show simple views by default, reveal depth on demand.
  DarkStart is calm. Complexity appears when you reach for it.

| Clear proximal goals
| Panel layout makes current task visible. The workspace _is_ the goal structure.
  You can see what you are working on without navigating away.

| Immediate feedback
| Live connection dots, health checks, proof status, build output — all visible
  without requesting them. Feedback is ambient, not modal.

| Elimination of distraction
| Full-screen panels, keyboard-driven navigation, no unnecessary chrome. The
  semicolons and brackets and boilerplate are handled by the tools so you can
  think about the problem.

| Sense of control
| Undo engine, reversible operations, session restore. You cannot break things
  irreversibly. Fear of breakage is a flow-killer.

| Intrinsic motivation
| Beautiful, well-crafted interface. Satisfying transitions. The tool itself is
  pleasant to use, not merely functional.
|===

=== What Breaks Flow (and When It Should)

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Interruption | When It Is Justified

| Build failure
| Always — this is the "misplaced gear". The system must pull you out clearly and
  help you recover quickly.

| Security alert
| Always — Hypatia/panic-attacker findings are genuine emergencies.

| Type error
| Always — but presented in-context, not as a wall of terminal output. The
  motorcycle swerves, it does not crash.

| Configuration demand
| Never during flow. Configuration should happen before or after a session, not
  during one.

| Authentication prompt
| Minimise. Pre-authenticate. Token refresh should be invisible.

| Network latency
| Hide it. Optimistic UI. Show stale data with a subtle staleness indicator
  rather than a spinner that blocks everything.
|===

=== The Motorcycle Checklist

Before shipping any PanLL feature, ask:

1. **Does this keep the rider on the road?** — Does the feature maintain flow or
   introduce a speed bump?
2. **Is the view beautiful?** — Does the UI feel crafted, or merely functional?
3. **Can I feel the wind?** — Is the feedback ambient and continuous, or do I
   have to stop and check?
4. **Am I fiddling with the controls?** — Does configuration happen once, or does
   it interrupt every session?
5. **Does the emergency brake work?** — When something goes wrong, does the
   system pull me out clearly and help me recover, or does it leave me confused?

== Relationship to NAFA

The NAFA journey planning app (for neurodiverse adventurers) embodies related
principles through a different lens: _psychological safety through clarity,
structure, and sensory awareness_. NAFA creates conditions for calm confidence.
PanLL creates conditions for absorbed creativity. Both reject the idea that
software should demand cognitive overhead as the price of functionality.

== Relationship to Productive Meandering

The Productive Meandering methodology (see `gossamer/docs/`) describes a mode of
exploration where serendipitous discovery is encouraged within guardrails. This
is the _countryside_ in the motorcycle metaphor — you have a direction but not a
rigid route, and the joy is partly in what you discover along the way. PanLL's
workspace model should support this: structured enough to maintain flow, open
enough to allow productive wandering.
